Chokro
Chokro is a circular-economy platform for Bangladesh. It helps people, schools, offices, collectors, recyclers, NGOs, and buyers give unwanted items a better next life.
Users can sell, donate, repair, reuse, or recycle clothes, books, plastics, paper, glass, metals, furniture, small appliances, and e-waste. The platform rewards verified sustainable actions with Green Credits, leaderboards, badges, vouchers, and cash-out requests.
Core ideas
* Five-path routing: Reuse, donate, repair, resell, recycle
* Circular marketplace: Sell reusable goods and recyclable materials
* Next-Life Agent: an orchestrated AI pipeline that reads an uploaded photo, calls a vision tool to detect category and condition, cross-references a live rate card for estimated value, and reasons through the best next-life path with a stated rationale — one system instead of three disconnected rules
* Trust Gate: an automated verification layer that auto-clears routine pickups and redemption requests, and escalates only genuine mismatches or anomalies to a human admin — so verification scales without scaling admin headcount 1:1 with users
* Verified recycling: Route e-waste and hazardous items to approved partners
* Green rewards: Earn credits after verified pickups or deposits
* QR drop zones: Collect materials at campuses, schools, offices, and apartments
* Community engagement: Compete through badges, challenges, and leaderboards
* Impact tracking: Show waste diverted, materials collected, and estimated environmental impact
* Partner network: Connect users with collectors, recyclers, repair shops, vendors, and NGOs

This preserves a coherent product: a user lists a plastic bag, old books, clothes, metal, furniture, or e-waste; the Next-Life Agent recommends the sustainable path; the user confirms; they connect with a partner; Trust Gate verifies the outcome; and they receive Green Credits that improve their personal or campus sustainability score.
Main project value
Chokro does not treat every unwanted item as garbage. It helps users choose the most sustainable next step, and it does the verification work that usually kills these platforms at scale:
* Good clothes → donate or resell
* Old books → donate, resell, or recycle
* Broken phone → repair, parts recovery, or e-waste recycling
* Plastic bottles → verified recycling
* Metal and steel → sell to collection partners
* Campus waste → QR deposit and pickup
The Next-Life Agent turns three disconnected rules into one reasoned recommendation with a visible rationale, which is also the technically defensible core of the project. Trust Gate turns manual, all-or-nothing admin verification into a triage system, so the platform can grow past a handful of admins without every pickup and redemption needing a human to click "approve." Together they combine sustainability, rewards, recycling operations, and community participation into one system that can plausibly scale.
